I design precision hardware for automated machines.

Mechanical engineer at Applied Materials in Santa Clara. My work is the mechanical side of automated production equipment: mechanisms, thermal and flow management, and the diagnostics that keep a fleet of machines behaving identically. Trained in mechanical engineering at Illinois and aerospace engineering at IIT Bombay.

The problems I find most interesting are the ones where heat, flow, and structure have to be solved together inside a single tightly toleranced assembly, and where the answer has to survive contact with a production floor. That is true of a deposition chamber, an actuator housing, or a robot joint.

The bands follow the interference colors of a thin oxide film, the way film thickness is read by eye.

Work

2024 to now

Mechanical Engineer II

Applied Materials, Santa Clara, California

I own mechanical subsystems on an automated multi station production platform from concept through release: heated structural stages, gas delivery assemblies, thermal shielding, and vacuum hardware. The work runs on first principles analysis, FEA and CFD checked against measured machine performance, and then on the parts that make hardware real: GD&T, tolerance stack ups, supplier first articles, and manufacturing ramp. The platform happens to do atomic layer deposition, but the engineering is precision mechatronics.

The piece I am proudest of is a thermal management redesign of a process chamber that materially improved output uniformity while cutting hardware cost. Alongside it, I lead root cause work on the failures that only show up on a fleet: intermittent faults on individual stations, station to station mismatch, and contamination sources on multi station tools. That is system identification and fault diagnosis on production automation, done with DOE and structured 8D rather than guesswork.

Two patent applications filed from this work.
